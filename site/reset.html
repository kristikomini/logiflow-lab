Forgotten password

Two steps: prove the account is yours with the recovery code you were given when you signed up, then choose a new password. It is also how you change a password you still know — the flow is the same either way, and it signs you out everywhere else.

🧒 The spare key you were given at the start

Most websites do this by post: you say “I forgot”, they send a letter to the address they have for you, and being able to open that letterbox is how they know it is really you. This site has no post office at all — it cannot send you anything, ever.

So instead, on the day you joined, it handed you a spare key and said keep this somewhere safe. Showing the spare key is how you prove who you are. Use it once and it stops working, and you get a new spare key on your way out — so somebody who saw it over your shoulder last year cannot use it now.

🎓 Why a reset is two requests and not one

POST /api/auth/forgot-password takes the username and recovery code and returns a reset ticket: 256 random bits, stored server-side as a SHA-256 hash, valid for fifteen minutes, usable once, and superseded the moment another is issued for that account. POST /api/auth/reset-password then spends the ticket on the new password.

That ticket is precisely what an emailed reset link carries in its query string. Every property above is one that a real breach has turned on at some point — tickets stored in the clear, tickets that never expire, tickets that can be replayed, and old tickets left live while the user requests another five. Splitting the flow in two is what lets the proof arrive by one channel and the new password by another; here the first channel is a code you already hold rather than a mailbox, and the shape is otherwise identical.

Completing a reset revokes every refresh token on the account. If the reason you are resetting is that somebody else got in, leaving their session alive would make the whole exercise decorative. Read it: src/LogiFlow.Academy.Api/Endpoints/AuthEndpoints.cs, and 15 · Securing an API.

⚠ Both errors here say the same thing on purpose

“No such account” and “wrong recovery code” come back as one identical message. This form is the only place on the site where somebody can probe with a username and no password at all, so if those two answers differed it would be a free membership list — type names, watch which ones come back differently.

The same reasoning runs through the sign-in form. It is the one place registration deliberately breaks with: a username is printed on the leaderboard, so “that name is taken” discloses nothing you could not read off the board, and withholding it would only leave people guessing why signing up failed.
