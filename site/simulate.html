Interview simulator

The viva asks you to produce an answer. This asks you to produce one in ninety seconds, without stopping, with the next question already coming — and it opens with “mi parli di lei” and closes with “ha domande per noi?”, because those get asked every time and prepared almost never.

Why the conditions matter more than the questions

🧒 Simple

You can play a piece perfectly at home and fall apart at the recital. Nothing about the notes changed — the room did. If you have only ever practised in the quiet version of the room, the loud one is a surprise.

This page is the loud room: a clock, no pausing, and a question you did not choose.

🎓 Professional

Retrieval practice works best when the practice conditions match the test conditions — the finding is usually called transfer-appropriate processing, and it is why the review deck interleaves topics instead of blocking them. The rest of this site gets the content conditions right: free recall on the viva, mixed topics on the review.

What it cannot reproduce one card at a time is the continuity. In a real round you answer badly, and then you answer the next one while still thinking about the last one. That recovery is a separate skill and it is trainable, but only by being put in the situation.

⚠ The trap this page cannot save you from

You mark yourself, so the score means whatever you want it to. That is why there is no percentage at the end — only a transcript, with what you said next to what the course says and how long you took. A number invites you to optimise it. A transcript makes you read what you actually produced.

Where the questions come from

The technical items are drawn from two places you have already seen: the 378 Golden rules behind the viva, and the 536 bank questions from the chapter tests — asked here without their options, which turns each one from a recognition question into a recall question. Grades feed the same spaced-repetition schedule as everywhere else, so a fumble here comes back in a few days.

The opening and closing questions are in Italian, with the English underneath. The technical vocabulary stays English either way — that is how the conversation actually runs in Modena and Bologna, and chapter 38 has more on the shape of the round.
