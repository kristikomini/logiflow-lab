Viva

Everything else on this site gives you four options. This does not. You get the rule, you produce the reason it is true, and only then do you see the written one — because “spiegami perché” has no options underneath it, and the gap between recognising an answer and being able to say one is where interviews are lost.

Why this exists

🧒 Simple

Picking the right answer out of four is like recognising a face. Explaining the rule out loud is like drawing that face from memory. Both feel like knowing, but only one of them is what somebody asks you to do.

So this page hides the answer until you have said yours. That is the entire trick.

🎓 Professional

Multiple choice measures recognition; an interview measures free recall, and the two come apart badly. Recognition is supported by the options on the page — remove them and a chunk of what felt like knowledge goes with them. Everything on this site until now has been the supported kind.

Free recall is also the harder practice, and harder practice is the point: the effort of producing an answer is what consolidates it. That is the same finding the review deck is built on, one step further along.

The weak spot is honest to name: you mark yourself, and people mark themselves generously. Two things push back. Your own answer stays on screen beside the written one, so you compare texts rather than feelings. And the terms the rule itself uses — rowversion, IQueryable, 409 — are checked against what you wrote and shown as hit or missed. That check is literal and cannot tell whether you were right, but “your answer never mentioned rowversion” is a fact, and facts are harder to wave away.

Marks feed the same SM-2 schedule as the quiz deck, in a separate pile: a rule you hedged comes back tomorrow, one you said cleanly comes back in four days, then in eleven. The deck is generated from course/GOLDEN-RULES.md, so every rule you drill here is a rule the course actually taught.
