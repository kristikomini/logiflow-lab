In italiano

The glossary gives you the words. This gives you the sentences — because the thing that goes wrong in a colloquio is not vocabulary. It is knowing the answer, knowing the words, and still not being able to assemble a sentence at speed, so you simplify the answer down to what your Italian can carry and sound like you know less than you do.

🧒 Simple

You can know every word in a language and still freeze when someone asks you a question. Knowing the words and having said the sentence before are different things, and only the second one survives being nervous.

🎓 Why English stays inside the Italian

Italian developers speak Italian with English technical nouns embedded, and they do not translate them: la query, il deploy, fare il merge, una race condition. Translating them is the marker of somebody who learned the vocabulary from a textbook rather than from a standup.

So the sentences below keep them in English on purpose. That is not laziness — it is how the language is used in this industry, in this region. The glossary is still where the term-by-term mapping lives, including the handful that genuinely are translated.

Capitolo per capitolo

The one to memorise

“Non l'ho mai usato in produzione. So che serve per…, ma non le racconto un'esperienza che non ho.”

Interviewers test for bluffing and they are good at it. One honest “I have not done that” buys credibility for everything else you say in the hour; one invented project spends it all at once, usually two questions later when they ask a follow-up.

⚠ Lei, not tu

Use lei until they offer tu — and in a tech company in Bologna many will, within the first minute. Starting formal and being invited to relax costs nothing. Starting informal with someone who did not invite it is the kind of small thing that gets remembered.
