Review

Every question you have ever answered on this site is scheduled here. Get one right and the gap before you see it again grows; get it wrong and it comes back tomorrow. Twenty minutes here is worth more than an hour of re-reading, and this is the part of the site that decides whether you still know chapter 6 in November.

Why this works

🧒 Simple

Imagine trying to remember a friend's phone number. If you look at it fifty times in one afternoon you will forget it by Friday. If you look at it today, then in two days, then in a week, then in a month — each time just before you would have forgotten — it sticks for years.

That is all this page does. It remembers which things you nearly forgot, and shows them to you at exactly the right moment.

🎓 Professional

Two findings from the learning-science literature do most of the work here. The first is the testing effect: being asked to retrieve something strengthens the memory far more than reading it again, even when the retrieval fails. The second is spacing: the interval between reviews should expand, because recall that requires effort is what consolidates.

The scheduler is SM-2, the algorithm behind SuperMemo and Anki, with one change that matters: a right answer you had to work for is graded differently from one you were sure about, so the interval grows more slowly. That is what the “I half-guessed” button is for, and skipping it honestly is the fastest way to make this page useless.

There is a third effect you get for free. Because the schedule is global rather than per chapter, a session mixes async with T-SQL with Docker — which is interleaving. Mixed practice feels harder and measurably outperforms studying one topic at a time, because it forces you to work out which tool applies before applying it. That is also exactly what an interview does.
